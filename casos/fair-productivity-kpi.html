From Warehouse Transactions to a Fair Productivity KPI

A warehouse system records every movement. Turning those movements into a productivity measure that is fair to each team and each operator takes more than a pivot table: shifts, rosters and real hours worked have to be joined to the transactions first.

About this page. This is an ETL I am building as part of a logistics process-improvement project in two distribution warehouses. It is work in progress, and the numbers below are synthetic. The company, the system vendor, the sites and the people are deliberately not named.

01 — The questionHow productive is each team, and each person in it?

The business question sounds simple: how many movements does a team complete per hour? The answer depends on two things the warehouse system does not hold by itself: who was actually working in each shift, and for how many hours. The system knows what moved and when, and which user did it. The roster knows who belonged to which team. The time and attendance record knows how long they were there. The ETL's job is to bring the three together.

One constraint shaped the design: there is no direct access to the warehouse database. Data arrives as exports, either downloaded by me from the transactions screen or extracted by IT on request. So the pipeline is built around files, and built so that replacing the manual export with an automated one changes only the first step.

03 — The metricOne KPI, specified before it was built

KPI specification

Name, formula, source, frequency, unit and alert threshold
FieldDefinition
NameMovements per real hour
Formula(loading movements + receiving movements) ÷ hours actually clocked
SourceWarehouse system transactions, shift roster, time and attendance record
FrequencyRefreshed weekly, read monthly
UnitMovements per person-hour
Alert thresholdTo be set after a baseline period, not before

Decisions that changed the number

  • Real hours, not theoretical hours.The first version divided by operators present multiplied by shift length, minus stoppages. Operations pointed out that this overstates the denominator, so the KPI now divides by hours actually clocked.
  • Team first, then person.Operations wanted productivity by team and, inside each team, by operator, so the model keeps the user on every movement and rolls up from there.
  • Net and gross, side by side.Monthly productivity is shown net (only the teams that worked) and gross (all four teams, including the one on rest), because the two answer different questions: how fast the working teams are, and what the whole structure delivers.
  • Presence comes from the data.An operator counts as present in a shift when they appear on movements in that shift, cross-checked against the roster instead of trusted blindly.
  • Team leaders stay in the denominator.They are part of the hours the shift consumes, so leaving them out would flatter the result.

05 — Where it standsWhat is built and what is next

  • Built or in progress.The KPI is defined and agreed with operations. The Excel dashboard is being built and refreshes weekly from the export. The shift roster exists, and a shift attendance log is being piloted.
  • Started with IT.Automating the extraction: the history already stored, plus a daily load of the previous day.
  • Next.Move the same model to Power BI when access is available, and add the data-quality checks the model needs: users in the transactions who are missing from the roster, movements without a timestamp or outside the three shift windows, duplicates created by overlapping weekly downloads, and people present with no hours clocked.
